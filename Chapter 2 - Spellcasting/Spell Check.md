To more neatly integrate spellcasting with contests, Spell checks are introduced. Whenever the effectivity of your spell is in question, you now make a Spell check to determine just how potent it currently is. This occurs in roughly three contexts: when you make a [[#Spell Attacks|spell attack]], when someone makes a [[#Spell Save DC|saving throw against one of your spells]], and when an [[#Antimagic|antimagical effect]] tries to prevent or end your spell.

**_Spell Check_**: To make a Spell check, you roll a D20 Test and add the spellcasting ability modifier relevant to the spell. If you aren't using a [[Spellcasting Focus]] that is associated with the school of the spell, it is unfocused, making you roll with Disadvantage.

**_Spell Check Proficiency_**: If you have the _Acuity_ [[Caster Training#Spell Check Features|feature]], you can add your Caster Proficiency Bonus to some Spell checks, otherwise, they are rolled without adding any Proficiency Bonus.
### Spell Attacks
For spell attacks, turning them into a contestable Spell check is quite straight forward, as attacks can already be contested through defensive Reactions. To streamline this, spell attack rolls are now defined as both a Spell check and an attack roll.

**_Replaces Spell Attack Rolls_**: When you make a spell attack, you now make a Spell check that also counts as an attack roll to determine whether the attack hits or not. If you have Advantage or Disadvantage on attack rolls, you also have Advantage or Disadvantage on this Spell check, respectively.
### Spell Save DC
For spells that require their targets to roll saving throws against a spell save DC, making them contest-like is also quite simple by making the Spell check determine the DC of the saving throw.

**_Replaces Spell Save DC_**: When a target of one of your spells makes a saving throw against it, you now make a Spell check to determine the DC. When multiple targets make this saving throw in the same turn, you only make one Spell check to determine the DC for all of them this turn.
### Antimagic
When one of your spells is faced with antimagical effects such as a _[[Spells#Counterspell|Counterspell]]_ or _[[Spells#Dispel Magic|Dispel Magic]]_, you now roll a Spell check to protect your spell, contesting the antimagical effect. Details are described within the antimagical effects themselves.
### Magic Items
Magic Items that previously gave a bonus to spell attack rolls and/or to the spell save DC now give that same bonus to Spell checks.
### Examples
The great Evoker Florb uses a Staff as a Spellcasting Focus to cast _Fireball_. As multiple targets now roll a saving throw, Florb makes a Spell check, rolling a D20 Test without Disadvantage, because their Staff focuses Evocation spells such as _Fireball_. They roll a 13 and add their Intelligence modifier of +4 for a total of 17, which is now the DC of the targets Dexterity saving throw.

Next round, Florb casts _Fire Bolt_. They roll their Spell check, adding their Intelligence modifier as before, for a total of 14. Thus, they hit their target if it has a Hit DC of 14 or lower, and if the target wants to reactively defend against the attack, they have to beat this 14.

Florb was not satisfied with that singular attack and casts _Scorching Ray_ in the next round. They roll an 11 on their first Spell check and thus the first spell attack, a 7 on their second one, and a 16 on their third one. Their Medium-sized target with a Hit DC of 10 is thus hit twice.

Due to the undesired attention they drew to themselves, Florb has to use _Greater Invisibility_. The rival Abjurer Evi wants to disrupt this and casts _[[Spells#Counterspell|Counterspell]]_. As Florb's Staff does not focus Illusion spells, they have to roll their Spell check to protect their spell with Disadvantage, rolling a 4 for a total of 20 given the specific rules for _[[Spells#Counterspell|Counterspell]]_, which is not enough to beat Evi's total of 26.
